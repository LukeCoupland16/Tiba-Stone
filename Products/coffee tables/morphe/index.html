The Morphe does not begin with a sketch — it begins with a slab. The veining of each stone is read like a topographic map: the lines, the pressure faults, the mineral inclusions. Form responds to material. The table that emerges is always singular, always shaped by what the stone itself proposes.

Viewed from above, Morphe reads as restraint. From the side, its profile reveals intention — an edge that is neither blunt nor precious, occupying the precise territory between mass and refinement. The table looks different in morning light than it does by evening. That variability is the point.
