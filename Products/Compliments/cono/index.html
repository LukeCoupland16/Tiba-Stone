Cono

lathe-turned marble cone

A lathe-turned marble cone — pure geometry realised in stone. The lathe reveals the stone's interior as no other process can: concentric rings of colour, uninterrupted veining, surfaces that taper to an impossible fineness.

The Form

Pure Geometry
in Stone

The cone is one of the oldest and most resolved geometric forms — it needs nothing added and nothing taken away. Cono realises this form in marble, turned on a lathe from a single block. The result is a surface that reads differently from every angle and catches light with extraordinary accuracy.

Presence & Weight

An Anchor
for Space

Cono pairs well with books, with vessels, with the edge of a shelf. It does not compete — it anchors. The base is wide enough to hold the eye and the apex is fine enough to feel like a question mark held in stone.

Stone & Form

The lathe process reveals each stone's interior differently. Verde Patricia produces a swirling, almost painterly surface; Calacatta Viola becomes dramatic and architectural. The form is constant; the material makes it singular.
