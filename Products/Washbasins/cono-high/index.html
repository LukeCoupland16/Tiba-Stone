Cono High

pedestal washbasin

Cono High takes the cone — the most resolved of three-dimensional forms — and elongates it to its full presence. Basin and column are inseparable; the geometry demands both.

The Form

The Cone,
Fully Resolved

The cone does not belong to architecture alone — it is a fundamental form of the natural world, of volcanic hills and crystal terminations. Cono High draws from both lineages: architectural in its precision, geological in its authority. The tall column is not a support; it is a continuation.

Geometry & Material

One Form,
One Stone

Column and basin are carved from a single block. The tapering geometry is maintained from base to rim without interruption. Veining that enters at the base reappears at the basin edge — the stone's narrative runs the full height of the piece.

Stone & Surface

Cono High's tapering geometry creates strong shadow lines that shift through the day. Stones with bold directional veining — Rosso Levanto, Noir Aziza — make these transitions dramatic. Ice Age Onyx's subtle patterning gives Cono High a more monolithic, sculptural quality.
